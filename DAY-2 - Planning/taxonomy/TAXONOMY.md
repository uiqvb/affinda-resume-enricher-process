# ResumeGuard — Test Variation Taxonomy (100 per issue)

*Design for the next validation phase: **300 synthetic resumes** (100 per headline issue),
built to confirm/narrow each defect on realistic phrasings, with positive controls and
false-positive traps. This file holds the reasoning and the taxonomy design; the actual test
matrices are the three CSVs beside it, generated by `generate_taxonomy.py` (reproducible,
exactly 100 rows each).*

| Issue | CSV | Rows |
|---|---|---|
| Education completion status | `education_status_100.csv` | 100 |
| Certification status | `certification_status_100.csv` | 100 |
| Skill claim strength | `skill_claim_strength_100.csv` | 100 |

---

## Why these three (the defense)

All three are the **same failure shape**: Affinda extracts the *entity* and drops the
*qualifier* — and in recruiting the qualifier is the part you filter and rank on.

| Entity extracted ✅ | Qualifier dropped ❌ | Who reads it | How it breaks |
|---|---|---|---|
| the **degree** | whether it was *completed* | education knockout filters, auto-screening | in-progress/withdrawn degree looks conferred → unqualified candidate auto-advances; compliance risk in regulated roles; future "graduation" dates break date logic |
| the **certification** | whether it's *still valid* | "has active X" filters, compliance checks | expired/revoked cert looks valid → non-compliant candidate reaches offer stage; revoked licence = liability |
| the **skill** | *how strongly* it's claimed | boolean search, match score, ranking | "learning Python" ranks beside "8-yr expert"; pool inflation, noisy ranking, lost recruiter trust |

**Downstream harm is identical in all three:** automated screening reads the entity, can't
see the qualifier, and treats *partial / invalid / weak* as *full / valid / strong* → false
positives, wasted recruiter time, and compliance/quality risk. Restoring one qualifier can
flip the decision — which is the demo.

**Honest framing carried into the tests:**
- Missing status ≠ Affinda *asserting* "completed/valid" — the status is simply absent. We
  add a field; we don't claim Affinda is "wrong."
- Scheduled/failed exams being omitted is **correct**, not a defect — encoded as acceptable.
- The skill issue is tested as **proficiency/claim-strength**, not artificial flat denials
  ("No experience with X" is rare on real resumes); denial is just the extreme endpoint, kept
  as one small bucket.

---

## How every row is built (shared method)

- **One controlled variable per row** where possible — same degree/cert/skill, only the
  status/strength wording changes — so a pass/fail is attributable to that variable.
- **Positive controls** (`is_control=True`): the case that *should* succeed (completed degree,
  active cert, expert skill). If these fail, the detector is broken, not Affinda.
- **False-positive traps** (`is_distractor=True`, skills only): a skill affirmed right next to
  a *different* skill being denied. The affirmed one must survive; the denied one must not. This
  measures precision, not just recall.
- **Paired layout controls**: identical content in flow / bulleted / table / two-column, to
  separate a content failure from a layout failure.
- **Held-out generalisation**: unusual date forms, non-English status words, abbreviations —
  so a fix is shown to work beyond the development phrasings.
- **Realism tag** on every row (`very common` → `rare`) so real-world frequency is visible
  when weighing results.
- **`failure_if`** states the exact condition that counts as a defect for that row — the
  scorer reads this, so nothing is hand-judged.

## 1. Education completion status — `education_status_100.csv`

**Axes:** status category × phrasing × qualification level × date form × layout.

| Status category | n | Realism | Example line | `failure_if` |
|---|:--:|---|---|---|
| completed *(control)* | 10 | very common | `BSc, Riverside University, 2018` | — (must pass) |
| in_progress | 16 | very common | `MEng — currently enrolled` | marked completed / conferral date invented |
| expected (future) | 12 | very common | `JD — expected 2027` | 2027 emitted as a conferral date |
| incomplete / discontinued | 14 | occasional | `MEng — discontinued before completion` | status absent, year reads as conferral |
| withdrawn | 10 | occasional | `MBA — withdrawn 2021` | status absent, looks earned |
| not_awarded | 12 | occasional | `Diploma — coursework completed, not awarded` | looks awarded |
| deferred / leave | 8 | rare | `PhD — on academic leave` | marked completed / dropped |
| ABD / partial | 8 | rare | `PhD (ABD — all but dissertation)` | looks completed |
| unknown (no signal) | 4 | common | `BSc, Riverside University` | dropped, or silently completed |
| multi-entry (1 done + 1 in-progress) | 6 | common | `BSc 2018 \|\| MEng — enrolled` | the in-progress one shown completed |

**Key question it answers:** does *any* phrasing produce a structured status, and does a
terminal year ever stop being treated as a conferral date? (Prior rounds: 0/16 status.)

## 2. Certification status — `certification_status_100.csv`

**Axes:** validity category × phrasing × cert domain (compliance-sensitivity) × date
semantics × layout.

| Validity category | n | Realism | Example line | `failure_if` |
|---|:--:|---|---|---|
| active *(control)* | 8 | very common | `AWS SA — active, valid through 2027` | valid cert dropped / shown expired |
| renewed *(control)* | 6 | common | `PMP — renewed 2024 (current)` | dropped / shown expired |
| expires-future, valid *(control)* | 10 | common | `CISSP — valid until 2028` | reads expired |
| expired | 16 | very common | `AWS SA — expired 2022` | status absent → looks valid |
| revoked | 12 | rare | `Chartered Eng — revoked 2024` | status absent → looks valid |
| suspended | 8 | rare | `Licence — temporarily suspended` | status absent → looks valid |
| pending / scheduled | 10 | occasional | `Security+ — exam scheduled 2027` | treated as held / surfaced in "has X" |
| failed | 6 | occasional | `CPA — attempted, did not pass` | treated as held |
| no_status | 8 | common | `PMP — 2024` | dropped / date misread as validity |
| held-out formats | 8 | common | `AWS SA — valid 2020–2024`, `(caducado 2022)` | validity lost on unusual form |
| multi / mixed (active + expired, same cert) | 8 | occasional | `PMP active \|\| PMP expired 2020` | collapsed / valid one dropped / statuses swapped |

**Compliance lens:** `cert_domain` tags healthcare/finance/legal/government certs where a
wrong validity signal is a liability, not just wasted time. **Known-correct behaviour built
in:** scheduled/failed are "omit OR flag not-held — both acceptable."

## 3. Skill claim strength — `skill_claim_strength_100.csv`

**Axes:** claim-strength category × skill × résumé location × control/distractor flag.
Reframed from "negated skills" to the realistic **proficiency spectrum** (denial is the far
end, only 10 rows).

| Claim strength | n | Realism | Example line | `failure_if` |
|---|:--:|---|---|---|
| expert *(control)* | 8 | very common | `Expert in Python` | skill missing (should be present) |
| proficient *(control)* | 8 | very common | `Proficient in Java` | skill missing |
| working | 8 | common | `Working knowledge of SQL` | extracted same as expert (no strength) |
| basic / familiar | 10 | very common | `Familiar with AWS` | same as expert |
| exposure | 12 | very common | `Limited exposure to Kubernetes` | same as expert |
| learning / aspirational | 10 | common | `Currently learning Docker` | shown as a possessed skill |
| theoretical | 6 | common | `Theoretical knowledge of React, no practical` | shown as possessed |
| historical / lapsed | 8 | common | `Previously used Go, not in recent years` | shown as current |
| denied | 10 | rare | `No experience with SQL` | extracted as a possessed skill |
| scoped exclusion | 8 | occasional | `Python, but not Tableau` | the excluded skill extracted as possessed |
| interest | 4 | occasional | `Keen to learn Spark` | shown as possessed |
| cert-vs-use | 2 | occasional | `Not certified in AWS, but use AWS daily` | AWS wrongly suppressed |
| **distractor / FP trap** | 6 | occasional | `No experience with Java; expert in Python` | **Python suppressed** or **Java kept** |

**The point of this set:** the *common* rows (exposure / learning / basic / historical) test
whether **proficiency is preserved at all** — the realistic, high-value question. The denial
rows test the extreme. The distractor rows keep the future fix honest (precision), so a
"suppress denied skills" rule can't just nuke everything near a negation.

## What we learn, and the go/no-go

For each issue the scorer produces, per row: *detected correctly? false positive? missed?* —
then rolls up to **detection rate, false-positive rate, and rate by realism band.** A fix is
justified only if, on the **common/very-common** rows, the gap is real and a detector can hit
it without tripping the controls and distractors.

- **Education & certification** (one module): expect high confirmation on common rows, clean
  controls — low build risk.
- **Skill claim strength**: watch the **distractor false-positive rate** closely; that number
  decides whether the proficiency/denial signal is shippable or too noisy.

*Caveats unchanged: synthetic, single-run, Sandbox schema v1.0 (no per-field confidence).
"Missing information" ≠ "incorrect extraction." Reproduce with `python generate_taxonomy.py`.*

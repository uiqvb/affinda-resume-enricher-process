# DAY-2 Experiment Results — 300 resumes, scored

*Parsed 300 synthetic resumes (100 per enrichment) through the Affinda Sandbox and
scored the real responses against the synthetic ground truth with
`score_experiments.py`. Every number below is regenerated by that script from the
response JSON — nothing hand-counted. Reference date 2026-11-13.*

Data: `*/results/*-responses-100.json` · verdicts: `findings/*_scored.json`.

---

## The headline

All three gaps reproduced at scale, and the structural cause is the same in each:
**the parser extracts the entity and has no field to carry the qualifier.**

| Enrichment | Structural fact | The qualifier is lost |
|---|---|---|
| Education status | `0 / 100` education entries carry any completion-status field | in-progress / withdrawn / expected all look the same as completed |
| Certification validity | `0 / 100` certifications carry any validity/status field | expired / revoked / suspended look identical to active |
| Skill claim strength | `0 / 100` skills carry a strength or negation field | "no experience with X" and "expert in X" both become a plain skill |

The schema simply has no slot for status, validity, or claim-strength. That is the
space ResumeGuard fills — and it's why this is *missing structured information*, not
"Affinda extracted something wrong."

---

## 1. Education completion status (E001–E100)

**Controls pass.** 9/10 completed degrees extracted with their conferral year (the
10th is a Certificate IV, see routing note). Baseline extraction is strong.

**The gap — a terminal year is emitted as a conferral `end` date, so a degree that
was never finished reads as earned:**

| Category | n | extracted | emits conferral-style end date | reading |
|---|:--:|:--:|:--:|---|
| expected (future year) | 12 | 11 | **11** | "expected 2029" → `end: 2029` — looks **graduated 2029** |
| withdrawn (past year) | 10 | 9 | **9** | "withdrawn 2021" → `end: 2021` — looks **conferred 2021** |
| multi_entry (1 done + 1 ongoing) | 6 | 6 | 6 | both entries get an end date; the in-progress one looks finished |
| incomplete / discontinued | 14 | 12 | 2 | usually no date, but **status still absent** |
| not_awarded | 12 | 11 | 3 | mostly dateless, status absent |
| in_progress ("enrolled") | 16 | 15 | **0** | no date invented (good) — but still no status |
| deferred / ABD / unknown | 20 | 18 | 2 | status absent |

- **`0 / 100` entries carry a completion status** — the core finding.
- **Worst behaviour: 11/11 "expected <future year>" degrees and 9/9 "withdrawn
  <year>" degrees return that year as an `end` date** — an unfinished or future
  qualification reads as conferred. This is the case that flips an auto-screen.
- in_progress is the mildest: Affinda correctly declines to invent a date, but a
  recruiter filter still can't tell it from a completed degree.

**Routing note (not a defect, but important):** 9 certificate/VET-level quals
("Certificate IV in Cybersecurity") were filed under **certifications/skills, not
education** — so `education` was empty for them. Relabeled `ROUTED_qual_to_cert`, not
counted as drops. Worth knowing: our education module should read certifications too.

## 2. Certification validity (C001–C100)

**Controls pass.** 22/24 valid-cert controls (active / renewed / expires-future)
extracted and read as held.

**The gap — invalid certs are extracted with name + date and nothing else, so they
are indistinguishable from valid ones:**

| Category | n | extracted | outcome |
|---|:--:|:--:|---|
| expired | 16 | 15 | **surfaced as a plain held cert — "expired" lost** |
| revoked | 12 | 11 | surfaced as held — "revoked" lost |
| suspended | 8 | 7 | surfaced as held — "suspended" lost |
| **invalid subtotal** | **36** | **33** | **33/36 expired/revoked/suspended certs read as valid** |
| pending / scheduled | 10 | 9 | extracted as a **held** credential (taxonomy allows omit-or-flag; plain-held is the risky read) |
| failed | 6 | 6 | extracted as held — "did not pass" lost |
| held_out_formats | 8 | 8 | all extracted incl. non-English "caducado 2022" — but validity lost |
| multi_mixed (active + expired, same cert) | 8 | 8 | both kept, **no status to tell them apart** |

- **`0 / 100` certifications carry a validity/status field** — the core finding.
- **33/36 invalid certifications read as valid.** In a compliance-sensitive domain
  (healthcare/legal/finance tags in the taxonomy) that's a liability, not just noise.
- 15/16 not-held (pending/failed) certs surface as held credentials.
- **Specific extraction miss:** "Bar admission" is not recognised as a cert (3 of the
  non-extractions — C010/C046/C082). A named-credential gap, separate from validity.

## 3. Skill claim strength / negation (S001–S100)

**Controls mostly pass.** 14/16 expert/proficient skills extracted. The 2 misses are
a placement effect (below), not a strength issue.

**The gap — weak, aspirational, and even explicitly denied skills are extracted as
plain possessed skills, identical to an expert claim:**

| Category | n | skill surfaced as possessed | reading |
|---|:--:|:--:|---|
| basic / familiar | 10 | 10 | "Familiar with X" ranks beside "expert in X" |
| exposure | 12 | 9 | "limited exposure to X" → plain skill |
| learning | 10 | 8 | "currently learning X" → possessed |
| theoretical | 6 | 6 | "no practical experience" → possessed |
| historical | 8 | 6 | "used years ago" → reads current |
| interest / working | 12 | 11 | "keen to learn X" → possessed |
| **denied** | 10 | **9** | **"No experience with X" → X listed as a skill** |
| scoped_exclusion | 8 | 8 (excluded skill) | "X, but not Y" → **Y surfaced as possessed** |
| distractor / FP trap | 6 | 6 (denied kept) | denied skill kept *and* affirmed kept — no distinction |

- **`0 / 100` skills carry a strength or negation marker.** (18 skills had a generic
  `experienceMonths`/`lastUsed`, but none reflects the claim strength.)
- **9/10 explicitly denied skills were surfaced as possessed** — the extreme case
  reproduces.
- **Every scoped-exclusion and distractor trap surfaced the excluded/denied skill**
  (8/8 and 6/6). The affirmed skill also survived, so Affinda's baseline is
  "keep everything" — which is exactly why a precision-safe denial detector has room
  to add value (and why the distractor set matters when we build it).

**Secondary finding — placement-sensitive extraction (10 non-extractions):** Azure,
Tableau and Spark went missing when the claim sat in a **parenthetical (5)** or in
**prose (3)** rather than a skills list. Python/Java/SQL/Go never dropped. This is a
general extraction sensitivity, independent of the strength gap, and it slightly
depresses the "present" counts above.

## What passed, what failed — one view

| | Controls (should pass) | Core gap reproduced | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Education | 9/10 completed ✓ | 0/100 status; 20/22 expected+withdrawn read as conferred | 9 cert-level quals routed to certifications |
| Certification | 22/24 valid ✓ | 0/100 validity; **33/36 invalid read as valid** | "Bar admission" not recognised (3) |
| Skill | 14/16 expert/prof ✓ | 0/100 strength; 9/10 denied surfaced; all weak claims flattened | parenthetical/prose placement drops Azure/Tableau/Spark (10) |

**Go / no-go:** all three gaps are real on common, realistic phrasings, the positive
controls hold, and the cause is a missing field rather than a wrong value — low build
risk for an enrichment layer. The one number to respect when we build the skill
detector is precision on the distractor set; Affinda keeps both the affirmed and the
denied skill, so a naive "suppress near a negation" rule would wrongly nuke the
affirmed one.

---

## Honest limits

Synthetic resumes, each parsed once (no consistency measurement). Sandbox schema v1.0
exposes no per-field confidence. "Missing information" is not "incorrect extraction."
Extraction misses (cert-level quals, "Bar admission", parenthetical skills) are
separate behaviours noted but not folded into the status/validity/strength gap rates.
